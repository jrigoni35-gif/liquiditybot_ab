# Who Loses to Us, Mechanically

## The synthesis
> **"We monetize forced and emotional flow, we stand down before informed flow, and the classifier
> between them is priced adverse selection plus THALES, not confidence."**

## Whom we monetize
1. **Attention-recruited late-cohort retail.** Price rises recruit *new* cohorts across 95 countries;
   roughly three-quarters are estimated to have lost money; larger holders sell into the inflow.
   Crucially: **"the loser pool is replenished, not educated"** — preferences are stable, and
   self-attribution bias defeats within-cohort learning. We are the resting counterparty (limit-only
   entries).
2. **Forced sellers in stop cascades.** "Flow is forced, hence uninformed at execution." It persists
   because "anchor stops are a coordination equilibrium."
3. **Disposition-effect holders**, whose winner-banking caps rallies at price magnets.

## Whom we refuse to fight
- **Insiders** pre-listing or pre-unlock — "never fade, never join late." The routine-vs-opportunistic
  split is "the most transferable" distinction: unlocks are routine, founder-wallet moves are
  opportunistic.
- **Herding institutions** — "fading it = trading against informed flow." Align or stand aside.
- **Spoofers** — veto entries aligned with the spoof-pushed direction.

## Why this is a strategy document, not a psychology document
It converts a literature review into an **admission rule**: for any flow, ask whether it is forced,
emotional, or informed. The first two are the edge; the third is the risk. And the classifier is
**priced** — adverse selection charged at the gate — rather than a confidence judgment.

## The mirror finding
The bot's own exit machinery is the **inversion** of the disposition effect: the ratchet rides winners,
protocol stops cut losers. The literature pass "supplies the citations the exit design was implicitly
resting on."
